# F264.1 — Warlord/CoM2 `SpellTypeGroup = 16` target selection: which record the gate reads

*Merged evidence, `CASTER.EXE`. Method B: two independent derivations
(`.derivations/F264.1.Claude.md`, `.derivations/F264.1.GPT.md`), reciprocal reviews
(`.reviews/F264.1.review-of-Claude.md`, `.reviews/F264.1.review-of-GPT.md`), one revision round each,
merged 2026-09-08. Brief: `.derivations/F264.1.BRIEF.md`.*

## Scope and outcome

**A `SpellTypeGroup = 16` (`SGUnitCurseNormalUnit`) spell's "normal unit only" gate reads the
calculated `Units[]` record. Its group-15 (`SGUnitBuffNormalUnit`) mirror reads the permanent
`BaseUnits[]` record.**

This answers F264 for the group-16 class. Targeting record choice is effect-specific:
these two classes read different records at their respective admission tests.

| Arm | Test site | Read site | Displacement | − base | Offset | Field | Record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `SpellGroup = 15` | `$0053DC9A` | `$0053DCC5` | `$01AC1867` | `$01AC1798` (`BaseUnits`) | `$CF` = 207 | `Fantastic` | **permanent** |
| `SpellGroup = 16` | `$0053DCF5` | `$0053DD20` | `$06426967` | `$06426898` (`Units`) | `$CF` = 207 | `Fantastic` | **calculated** |

Both write `SPTMustBeNormal` = `-4` = `$FFFFFFFC`.

`flatten(UNITT)` (`tools/caster_record_layout.py`) gives `('Fantastic', 207, 1, 1)`, so both sites
name the **same field of the same record type**; the only difference is the array base. The
identification is forced rather than judged: the cross-assignment `$06426967 − $01AC1798 =
$049651CF` lies far outside the 1,924-byte record stride, so no other resolution is arithmetically
available.

Both derivations reached this independently, and each reviewer re-derived it from the binary rather
than accepting it.

## Record-base provenance

`tools/annotate_caster_disasm.py` carries `UNITS_BASE, BASEUNITS_BASE, UNIT_SIZE = 0x6426898,
0x1AC1798, 1924`, and `Combat.ApplyAttack.R5.2a-b.evidence.md:51-53` records the same bindings — but
both are reused bindings, not evidence produced here. The independent binary confirmation is a pair
of TD32-named accessors, each copying one whole record out of one named array:

```
@Castercore@GetUnitData      $006473A1  lea esi,[edx+eax*4+$06426898]
                             $006473AA  mov ecx,$1E1   $006473AF  rep movsd
@Castercore@GetBaseUnitData  $006473ED  lea esi,[edx+eax*4+$01AC1798]
                             $006473F6  mov ecx,$1E1   $006473FB  rep movsd
```

The routine names bind the arrays; the `rep movsd` count binds the stride (`$1E1` dwords = 1,924
bytes = the `imul` literal at every indexed site in this extent). Their base difference,
`$04965100 = 40000 × $784`, is exactly one declared array.

Both are displacement constants used with an unadjusted one-based index, not absolute element-1
addresses. Every indexed access has the shape `Mem32($00709188) + u × $784 + disp`.

Declared bounds, from the declarations rather than from loop inference:
`BaseUnits, Units : array[1..maxunitslots+1] of UnitT` (`Typedec.pas:336`) with
`Maxunitslots = 39999` (`SharedConstants.pas:14`) → `1..40000`, matching the omitted guard's
`u − 1 <= $9C3F`; and `EnchFlagT = array[1..maxmaxenchantmentflag] of boolean` (`Typedec.pas:147`)
with `MaxMaxEnchantmentFlag = 100` (`SharedConstants.pas:29`) → `1..100`, matching
`enchantflag − 1 <= $63`.

## The read convention, and the asymmetry

Sorted by kind, the ladder is internally consistent in every category except one:

| Kind of field | Sites | Record |
|---|---|---|
| roster-derived scalars: `experience`, `ishero`, `createoutpost`, `regeneration`, `magicimmunity`, `missileImmunity`, `flying`, `overlandmaxmoves`, `combatmaxmoves` | 9 | permanent, without exception |
| persistent enchantment layers: `OverlandEnchantmentFlags`, `CombatEnchantmentFlags` | 2 | permanent, both |
| `owner` | 2 | calculated, both |
| calculated enchantment layer: `EnchantmentFlags` | 4 | calculated, all four |
| `Fantastic` | 6 | **permanent at 3 sites, calculated at 3** |

`Fantastic` is the only field in the routine read from both records, and the normal-unit pair is
where it splits. `owner` reading calculated is not a counter-example: combat can transfer control, so
"who owns it now" is the calculated question, and both sites agree.

### Suspected engine defect — reported as such, not normalised

The two arms occupy `$0053DC7B..$0053DCD6` and `$0053DCD6..$0053DD31`, **91 bytes each**,
instruction-for-instruction identical, differing in exactly two immediates: the group compared
(`$0F` / `$10`) and the array base. `SGUnitBuffNormalUnit = 15` and `SGUnitCurseNormalUnit = 16` are
adjacent constants and are the buff and curse halves of one stated predicate. A rule under which the
buff half judges normality from the permanent record while the curse half judges it from the
calculated one is stated nowhere in the constants, the INI files, or the ladder.

**The boundary of this claim matters.** Re-disassembly establishes the mirrored predicates and the
differing displacements. It supplies no author intent and no editing history. Neither derivation
claims which of the two arms is the intended one, and an earlier "cannot both be intended" formulation
was withdrawn under review as asserting more than a disassembly can carry.

**What the difference actually is:** where the two `Fantastic` copies hold different values, the
group-15 and group-16 arms disagree about whether to assign `SPTMustBeNormal`. That is all. It is not
a claim that the unit becomes a legal target — `Result` is assigned by a ladder with **no early
exits**, the ownership arms run first, twelve later arms can overwrite it, and the routine continues
past `$0053E216` into a realm/immunity tail this subunit does not cover. The *last* matching arm
wins, not the first.

**Whether the defect is live or latent is unresolved.** Nothing in this extent shows that
`Units[].Fantastic` and `BaseUnits[].Fantastic` can ever differ; settling that means reading the
`Fantastic` write ladder in `@Units@RecalculateUnits`, outside this extent. Nothing above depends on
its answer.

### Second suspected defect — `$0053E0A1`

```
$0053E0A1  cmp dword ptr [ebp-$0C], 2      ; sp = 2   (Resist Elements, both builds)
$0053E0A7  cmp dword ptr [ebp-$08], $51    ; u  = 81
$0053E0AD  mov dword ptr [ebp-$10], -7     ; SPTAlreadyHasAbility
```

`[ebp-$08]` is the **unit** parameter — the same slot that feeds every `Units[u]` index computation
in the routine. This arm reads **no unit field at all**, the only "already has ability" test in the
ladder that does not, and every neighbouring arm has the shape
`if sp = <id> then if <unit field> then Result := …`. 81 is a *spell* id in both builds (Animate
Ammo; `SharedConstants.pas` still carries the stale name `SWarpWood = 81`), but that coincidence
identifies nothing: **the intended expression is unresolved and neither derivation offers one.**

## Routing

The combat head admits a spell to the unit-target path on `SpellGroup ∈ {1, 15, 13, 16, 14}` or
`sp ∈ {5, 68, 89, 125, 148}`. A group-16 spell reaches the unit gate only after
`@Combat@Isvalidcombattile`, `u > 0`, and `@Combat@Cansee(w, u)`.

Two redundancies worth recording, neither a defect: **id 5 (Web) is group 14**, so the group-14 test
at `$0053C85A/$0053C85F` has already matched and its id branch is dead in both shipped
configurations; and in `ValidUnitSpellTarget`'s enemy ladder, ids 5 and 67 are similarly redundant.
Both branches are preserved in the reconstructions as compiled.

Ownership is a separate test from the normality test. **Neither reads race or unittype.**

## The group-16 spell set

Three entries in each build's `spells.ini`:

| Build | Group-16 spells |
|---|---|
| Base CoM2 1.05.11 | `[88]` Shatter, `[172]` Possession, `[230]` Mislead |
| Warlord 1.5.12.9 | `[88]` Rust, `[172]` Possession, `[230]` Liability |

**Warlord Shatter is not in this class.** Warlord moves Shatter to id 272 (Warlord `MASTER.CAS`, `SShatter = 272;`), which
carries `SpellTypeGroup = 13`, so it never reaches the group-16 arm. Base-CoM2 Shatter (id 88) does.
The F264 item body's claim that this reconstruction "settles Rust, Shatter and every future targeting
gate at once" is therefore wrong for Warlord Shatter.

Possession (172) is tested twice — by the group-16 arm and by its own id at `$0053DDB3` — both
reading `Units[u].Fantastic` and both writing `-4`. The duplicate is redundant *because* `spells.ini`
puts Possession in group 16; a configuration that re-grouped it would leave the per-id arm as the
only one that fires.

## Callers — the AI and the human share the gate

```
human combat:   @Ui@SpellTargetingUI -> CombatSpellTargetingHandle $006D7504
                  -> @Castercore@ValidCombatSpellTarget $0064BDB0   (pure thunk, no transformation)
                  -> @Spelltargeting@ValidCombatSpellTarget $0053C754
                  -> @Spelltargeting@ValidUnitSpellTarget $0053DB78
AI:             twelve @Aicombatspelltargeting@* routines call $0053C754 directly
cursor:         @Rendering@RenderMouseCursor calls the same wrapper
```

`CombatSpellTargetingHandle` is a Delphi **nested procedure** of `@Ui@SpellTargetingUI` (`push ebp;
call`), writing its parent's `targetingresult` local. The parent pushes EBP at `$006D7DBB`, calls at
`$006D7DBC`, pops ECX at `$006D7DC1`, compares `targetingresult` at `$006D7DC2` and branches signed
at `$006D7DC6`. That is what establishes this as the **human cast-time** input path rather than
cursor decoration.

The handler stores `targetingresult` and then sets the target location and unit **even for a negative
result**; the parent decides whether casting proceeds.

## Two confirmed defects in `tools/annotate_caster_disasm.py`

Both found in derivation and independently confirmed in review. A reader diffing this document
against a raw annotated dump will otherwise see contradictions.

1. **The two variable-index enchantment reads are labelled one array too low.** `$0053E050` is
   `OverlandEnchantmentFlags[enchantflag]` and `$0053E090` is `CombatEnchantmentFlags[enchantflag]`,
   both on `BaseUnits`; the tool resolves by displacement alone and ignores the register-supplied
   index. Its labels for the four constant-index reads are correct.
2. **Every `SpellGroup` test is labelled `W.Retorts[17=Charismatic]`.** The tool's small-displacement
   heuristic resolves `+$2C` against the wizard stride, but the base is the spell table, so `$2C` is
   `SpellTableT.SpellGroup`.

A fix should incorporate pointer provenance and index contributions before naming a field. No tool
file was changed by this reconstruction.
