# F225.1 — the two DOS gaze kill loops: double-count and bucket

Adjudication only. Read against the three shipped `WIZARDS.EXE` images (hashes in
`Reference docs/MoM binary analysis.md`, *The binaries*) and against `combat.c`'s
`BU_ProcessAttack` reconstruction. No calculator file is changed here; F225.2 executes.

## 1. The region is byte-identical in all three builds

`0x99D73`–`0x99EB0` — the stoning-gaze block, the death-gaze block and the fallthrough into the
Black Sleep test — decodes to the same bytes in MoM 1.31, MoM CP 1.60 and CoM 1 6.08. Verified two
ways: by disassembling all three at `0x99D73` for `0x150` bytes with `tools/scan_mom_binary.py dis`
(every instruction, operand and displacement matches), and by hashing the slice — all three give
SHA-256 `03B7148043E5E3A92E2AD60C8E32624334C1A510A42689D856F907709DCFDBB7`, with the three
whole-file md5s reproducing the table in `MoM binary analysis.md`. That agrees with `combat.c`'s
`160:=  com1:=` annotations on every statement in the block.

**Neither loop itself differs between builds.** Everything in §2 and §3 therefore holds identically
for `mom131`, `mom160` and `com1`. One build difference *adjacent* to them does change when the
charges land, and is recorded in §3.1.

The claim is scoped to `0x99D73`–`0x99EB0`. The bytes immediately *before* it are not identical:
the Immolation block at `0x99D3D`–`0x99D70` is where the three builds diverge (1.31 has no
`attack_mode <= am_Melee` gate, CP 1.60 adds one behind seven NOPs, CoM 1 rewrites the block and
leaves a dead `ATT_SUPERNATURAL` arm). None of that reaches either kill loop.

CoM 1's frame is larger — `sub sp,0x34` at `0x999CC` against 1.31's `sub sp,0x2e` — so identical
`bp` displacements are not by themselves proof of identical locals. They are confirmed
independently for CoM 1 in §2 below: CoM 1's *own* code takes `lea ax,[bp-0x28]` as the base of the
three-word `local_damage` array at `0x9A549`/`0x9A561`. The six extra bytes sit deeper in the
frame, below `[bp-0x28]`.

Both kill loops sit under one guard: `test word es:[bx+0x18], 0x20` at `0x99D3F`
(`USA_IMMUNITY_MAGIC`), whose taken arm jumps to `0x99EB0` past both. A Magic-Immune defender takes
neither gaze kill.

## 2. What each loop is

```
099D73  cmp word [bp-0xe], 0x67   ; ranged_type == RAT_STONING_GAZE (103)
099D79  cmp word [bp-0xe], 0x68   ; or RAT_MULTIPLE_GAZE (104)
099D8F  test word es:[bx+0x18], 2 ; defender Attribs_1 & USA_IMMUNITY_STONING -> skip
099D97  mov  word [bp-2], 0       ; k = 0
099D9E  xor  ax,ax / push          ; realm argument 0 = sbr_Nature
099DAE  mov  al, es:[bx+0x15]      ; attacker Spec_Att_Attrib
099DBA  neg  ax                    ; -abs(modifier)
099DD8  call 0x98FCB               ; Combat_Resistance_Check
099DE0  jle  0x99DF7               ; margin <= 0 -> no kill
099DEF  mov  al, es:[bx+0x10]      ; defender hits (HP per figure)
099DF4  add  word [bp-0x24], ax    ; local_damage[2] += hits
099DF7  inc  word [bp-2]
099E07  mov  al, es:[bx+0xd]       ; defender Cur_Figures
099E0F  jg   0x99D9E               ; k < Cur_Figures -> next roll
```

```
099E11  cmp word [bp-0xe], 0x68   ; RAT_MULTIPLE_GAZE (104)
099E17  cmp word [bp-0xe], 0x69   ; or RAT_DEATH_GAZE (105)
099E2D  test word es:[bx+0x18], 0x40 ; defender Attribs_1 & USA_IMMUNITY_DEATH -> skip
099E35  mov  word [bp-2], 0       ; k = 0
099E3C  mov  ax,4 / push           ; realm argument 4 = sbr_Death
099E4D  mov  al, es:[bx+0x15]      ; the SAME attacker Spec_Att_Attrib byte
099E59  neg  ax
099E77  call 0x98FCB               ; Combat_Resistance_Check
099E7F  jle  0x99E96
099E8E  mov  al, es:[bx+0x10]      ; defender hits
099E93  add  word [bp-0x28], ax    ; local_damage[0] += hits
099E96  inc  word [bp-2]
099EA6  mov  al, es:[bx+0xd]       ; defender Cur_Figures
099EAE  jg   0x99E3C
```

`local_damage` is the three-word array based at `[bp-0x28]`. The base is fixed by the flush loop's
`lea ax,[bp-0x28]` at `0x9A549`/`0x9A561`, by the summation loop's `lea ax,[bp-0x28]` at
`0x9A398`, and by CoM 1's `lea cx,[bp-0x28]` (`8D 4E D8`) at `0x99D40` where the address is passed
to the Immolation helper. Word stride, so:

| bp offset | index | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `[bp-0x28]` | 0 | regular |
| `[bp-0x26]` | 1 | undead |
| `[bp-0x24]` | 2 | irreversible |

CoM 1's larger frame adds locals *below* the array, at `[bp-0x30]` and `[bp-0x32]` — for example
`or word [bp-0x30], 1` at CoM 1 `0x99EC9`, the attack marker. It does not move `local_damage`.

Index 1 = undead is fixed by the Create Undead branch: `FF 46 DA` (`[bp-0x26]`, index 1) at
`0x9A365` on the Create-Undead-true arm and `FF 46 D8` (`[bp-0x28]`, index 0) on the else arm at
`0x9A36A`.

Index 2 = irreversible is fixed downstream, twice over:

- `Battle_Unit_Heal` computes `healable = damage[DMG_UNDEATH] + damage[DMG_REGULAR]` at
  `0x7FCC5..0x7FCF2` (CP 1.60's variant at `0x7FCC5..0x7FCE0`) — bucket 2 is the one healing
  cannot reach.
- `BU_ApplyDamage`'s death-disposition ladder decides a destroyed unit's fate by which bucket is
  largest: irreversible ≥ both → `BUS_GONE` (`0x87584`, `0x875AA`, write `0x875B9`); undead
  largest → `BUS_DRAINED`/`BUS_GONE` (`0x875E5`, `0x8760B`); regular largest → `BUS_DEAD`
  (`0x8767B`, `0x876A1`, write `0x876B0`). Only bucket 2 produces the unraisable outcome, which
  is what "irreversible" names.

A side effect of the ladder worth recording, though the calculator does not model post-combat
disposition: the two comparisons that select `BUS_GONE` are both `>=`, so a Multiple-Gaze kill in
which stoning and death charge equally leaves irreversible tied with regular and the unit is
**GONE**, not merely dead. Out of scope here (project CLAUDE.md, *Out of scope*: army resolution,
overland play), but it is a third independent confirmation of the mapping.

## 3. Ruling on the double-count: **charged twice**, identically in all three builds

The two loops are strictly sequential and mutually unconditional. The stoning loop's back edge at
`0x99E0F` falls through to `0x99E11`, the death loop's type test; every exit from the stoning block
converges on `0x99E11`, and the death block's entry conditions do not consult anything the stoning
block wrote. Nothing the stoning body calls can touch the defender either: `abs` (raw `0x02CC8`),
the by-value record copier (raw `0x03187`) and `Combat_Resistance_Check` (`0x98FCB`) write neither
`Cur_Figures` nor `status`.

Three facts settle it:

1. **Both loops are bounded by the same, unchanged `Cur_Figures`.** `0x99E07` and `0x99EA6` both
   re-read `es:[bx+0xd]` each iteration, and no instruction between `0x99D97` and `0x99EAE` writes
   that field. Figures are removed only in `BU_ApplyDamage` (`0x874D3`), which the caller runs
   after `BU_ProcessAttack` returns.
2. **Neither loop identifies a figure.** Each is `for k in 0..Cur_Figures-1: roll; if fail, add
   hits`. There is no per-figure state, so there is nothing that could suppress a second charge.
3. **Both writes survive.** `local_damage[2]` and `local_damage[0]` are summed into
   `target_damage` at `0x9A393..0x9A3A9` and then flushed into the caller's `damage_types` at
   `0x9A544..0x9A55A`, each index into its own bucket, with no clamp inside `BU_ProcessAttack`.

So for a Multiple Gaze (type 104) against an `n`-figure defender with per-figure stoning failure
`p_s` and death failure `p_d`:

- `S ~ Binomial(n, p_s)` and `D ~ Binomial(n, p_d)`, **statistically independent under the
  calculator's IID-roll abstraction**. That abstraction is the project's throughout, not something
  this ruling introduces: the executable draws all `2n` rolls from one deterministic PRNG state
  (raw `0x0FFA8`), so literal independence is not established by the binary and is not claimed
  here. What *is* established from the binary is the structural fact that decides F225 — two
  separate charges rather than one;
- `local_damage[2] += S · hits` (irreversible), `local_damage[0] += D · hits` (regular);
- the record's gaze contribution is `(S + D) · hits`, which for `n` figures can reach `2n · hits`
  — twice the defender's whole HP pool.

The overcharge is visible in **both** figures killed and `bu->damage[]`.

`BU_ApplyDamage` derives the kill count from the *sum* of the three buckets:
`figures_lost = total / bu->hits` at `0x8747E`, where `total` is `damage_types[0..2]` plus the
carried `front_figure_damage`. The clamp at `0x874D3` caps `figures_lost` at `Cur_Figures` and so
bites only in overkill — once the charge already exceeds the defender's whole remaining pool.
Below that threshold every extra `hits` charged is one extra figure killed, so the second charge
raises expected kills across the entire normal range. On `doomGazeChaosSpawn` (4 figures x 5 HP,
per-loop fail 0.2) expected figures killed is 1.588 under the engine's `S + D` against 1.440 under
a one-charge-per-figure model, and the clamp engages with probability 0.056.

It is separately visible in `bu->damage[]`, which is written per bucket and capped only at
`BU_DAMAGE_CEILING` 200 per bucket (`0x873F8`/`0x87409`/`0x8743F`). That per-bucket split
survives independently of the kill count and is what reaches the disposition ladder and DOS
healing. A unit that fails both rolls on every figure ends the attack carrying its full HP pool in
bucket 2 *and* its full HP pool in bucket 0.

Whether this was intended is not something binary reconstruction can settle, so it is not asserted
here. The project reproduces the behaviour either way (project CLAUDE.md: *Game bugs are reproduced
faithfully*).

### 3.1 One per-build difference: MoM 1.31 can discard both charges

Immediately after the two loops, at `0x99ECE`, all three builds compare `attack_strength`
(`[bp-0x12]`) against 0. The builds then diverge:

| build | `0x99ED2` | effect |
|---|---|---|
| MoM 1.31 | `7F 03` `jg 0x99ED7` | falls through to `0x99ED4` `jmp 0x9A581`, the epilogue |
| MoM CP 1.60 | `EB 03` `jmp 0x99ED7` | unconditional; the abort is dead |
| CoM 1 6.08 | `EB 03` `jmp 0x99ED7` | unconditional; the abort is dead |

`0x9A581` is the function epilogue, reached **before** the summation at `0x9A393` and the flush at
`0x9A544`. So in MoM 1.31 a gaze whose shared strength byte is non-positive completes both kill
loops and then throws the entire `local_damage` record away — both charges, not one. CP 1.60 and
CoM 1 keep them.

This qualifies the ruling's reach but not its content: it changes *whether* the charges land, never
*how many* there are. The calculator already models it — `hiddenGazeLevelLadderNeedsStrength`
(`presets_ranged_and_haste.js:834`) pins 0 for exactly this gate — and neither F225 fixture is
affected, since both carry positive strength.

### Only type 104 can reach both loops

Stoning fires on 103 or 104, death on 104 or 105. Both loops therefore run only for
`RAT_MULTIPLE_GAZE`. The double-charge is confined to that one ranged type — on the shipped
rosters, Chaos Spawn.

### The two failure probabilities share one input byte but need not be equal

Both loops read the same attacker field `Spec_Att_Attrib` (`es:[bx+0x15]`, `0x99DAE` and
`0x99E4D`) and pass `-abs()` of it. Only the realm argument differs: `0` (Nature) at `0x99D9E`,
`4` (Death) at `0x99E3C`. Any realm-specific resistance the defender carries can therefore make
`p_s ≠ p_d` even though the modifier byte is shared. Do not collapse them to one probability.

## 4. `buildGazeDist`'s joint model is a defect, not a deviation

`Calculator/combat_special_attacks.js:384-387` computes
`jointFail = 1 − (1 − stoningFail)(1 − deathFail)` and convolves **one**
`calcFigureKillDmgDist(defAlive, jointFail, def.hp)`. Its stated reason — "a figure dies once even
if it fails both" — is true of the *figure count* but false of the *damage record*, and the damage
record is what the calculator publishes.

It is not a *deviation* in the sense the project CLAUDE.md's *Deliberate deviations* section uses:
a deviation is a named choice made at a fork where the faithful option was available and rejected.
No such choice was recorded; the joint form was adopted as a reading of the engine, and the
reading is wrong. It is a plain faithfulness defect, which is the category F225 already carries.

The modern arm is unaffected: `buildAttackerGazePhase` / `buildDefenderGazePhase` call
`buildGazeDist` once per gaze type with the other failure passed as `0`, so `jointFail`
degenerates to the single probability.

### The shape F225.2 should implement

Two independent binomials, not one joint binomial, and two buckets:

```
stoningKill = calcFigureKillDmgDist(defAlive, stoningFail, def.hp)   -> irrecoverableDamage
deathKill   = calcFigureKillDmgDist(defAlive, deathFail,   def.hp)   -> normalDamage
```

both over the **same** `defAlive`, convolved independently onto the physical/doom base. Two
constraints on how it is wired:

- **Do not sequence it like the modern arm.** `sequenceTouchApplyAttacks` recomputes the surviving
  figure count between steps, which is right for Caster (see §6) and wrong for DOS, where both
  loops read one unchanging `Cur_Figures`. The DOS gaze is a single `BU_ProcessAttack` producing
  one three-bucket record.
- **The natural fit is `convolveTouchAttacks`'s existing rider channel.** `addDamage(riderDist,
  category, riderKey)` (`combat_fear_and_touch.js:249`) already convolves a distribution while
  attributing it to a named category and its own histogram. Leaving the base `dist` as
  physical + doom + death-kill (all `normalDamage`) and passing the stoning kill as a rider with
  category `irrecoverableDamage` and a new `stoningGaze` rider key gives both the bucket split and
  the per-rider histogram the *Architecture* section requires, and matches the `stoningGaze` row
  F225.2 restores in `runRiderHistogramChecks`. `stoningGaze` would have to be added to
  `TOUCH_RIDER_KEYS` (`combat_fear_and_touch.js:155`).

Open for F225.2, not settled here: whether the death-gaze kill should also become its own rider
histogram. The engine gives it no separate bucket (it shares regular damage with the physical and
doom components), but *Architecture* says each rider that contributes damage inside a phase gets
its own histogram. That is a presentation question, not a source question.

## 5. Fixture arithmetic under the ruling

Both fixtures live in `Calculator/presets_ranged_and_haste.js`; both are DOS-default (MoM 1.31).
The clip convention is the project's: the phase publishes `min(total, HP the target had entering
the phase)` — `shownDamage` at `Calculator/combat_state.js:321`, applied on the way out and nowhere
earlier.

Reproducing the *current* expectations from the current joint model confirms the pipeline model
used below: 9.640 and 11.133 both come out exactly.

### `combinedStoningDeathGaze` — number **unchanged at 9.640**, `desc` must change

Attacker: type 104, shared strength 1, 1 figure, modifier −3. Defender: 1 figure, 10 HP, Res 5 →
effective Res 2 → per-loop failure 0.8. Doom 1 is exact.

Under the ruling the phase-internal distribution is `1 + 10·S + 10·D`, `S,D ~ Bernoulli(0.8)`:

| S+D | probability | phase-internal | published (cap 10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.04 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 0.32 | 11 | 10 |
| 2 | 0.64 | 21 | 10 |

`E = 0.04·1 + 0.96·10 = 9.640`. The 10 HP cap absorbs the entire difference from the joint model
(which gave 1 w.p. 0.04 and 11 w.p. 0.96 — the same after clipping). **`expected.dmgToB` stays
9.640.**

The `desc` is nonetheless wrong and must be rewritten, because the arithmetic it states
(`1−(1−0.8)(1−0.8) = 0.96`, `0.96×10 + 1 = 10.60 inside the phase`) is the joint model. The
phase-internal expectation is `1 + 8 + 8 = 17`, and the split is irreversible 8 / regular 9
before clipping.

This fixture is therefore **not a guard** on the change: it passes either way. F225.2 should say so
in the `desc` and rely on `doomGazeChaosSpawn` plus a composition assertion for the guard.

### `doomGazeChaosSpawn` — **11.133 → 11.716**

Attacker: type 104, shared strength 4, 1 figure, modifier −4. Defender: 4 figures × 5 HP = 20 HP
pool, Res 12 → effective Res 8 → per-loop failure 0.2. Doom 4 is exact.

Ruling: `4 + 5·N` with `N = S + D ~ Binomial(8, 0.2)` (sum of two independent `Binomial(4, 0.2)`),
clipped at 20.

| N | probability | phase-internal | published |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.16777216 | 4 | 4 |
| 1 | 0.33554432 | 9 | 9 |
| 2 | 0.29360128 | 14 | 14 |
| 3 | 0.14680064 | 19 | 19 |
| ≥4 | 0.05628160 | ≥24 | 20 |

`E = 11.7162496` → **`expected.dmgToB: 11.716`**.

Phase-internal expectation `4 + 4 + 4 = 12`; split irreversible 4 / regular 8 before clipping.

## 6. Downstream consequences — both confirmed

**Damage-type composition readout: confirmed.** Today the DOS arm of `buildAttackerGazePhase`
(`Calculator/combat_phases.js:991`) passes no `baseDamageCategory`, so
`convolveTouchAttacks` books the whole gaze as `normalDamage`
(`combat_fear_and_touch.js:229-245`) and a stoning gaze prints `Regular 100.0%`. Under the ruling
the stoning kill is irreversible. A caveat F225.2 inherits: because the published phase total is
clipped at the target's HP while the riders are not (project CLAUDE.md, *Architecture*), an
overkill cell's buckets sum to more than the published total, so the composition needs a stated
rule for how it normalises there. This ruling does not settle that.

**DOS combat healing: confirmed, and the prompt's formula is the modern one.** DOS
`Battle_Unit_Heal` computes `healable = damage[DMG_UNDEATH] + damage[DMG_REGULAR]`
(`combat.c:2917`, `131:0x7FCC5..0x7FCF2  160:0x7FCC5..0x7FCE0  com1:=`) and clamps
`healing` to it at `0x7FCFD`/`0x7FD05`. Irreversible is excluded by omission rather than by
subtraction; the effect is the one F225 names —
stoning-gaze damage currently sitting in the regular bucket is healable when the engine would not
heal it. `Totaldamage − Irrecoverabledamage` is Caster's spelling
(`Combat.DamageHandling.pas:80,84`, mirrored at `Calculator/engine.js:351`).

## 7. The modern side — confirmed correct, no change proposed

`Combat.PerformAttacks.pas:162` and `:177` issue two independent `ApplyAttack` calls,
`ATStoningGaze` and `ATDeathGaze`. Inside `ApplyAttack` each type gates its own loop:
`ATDeathGaze` writes `Result.field_08` (`Combat.ApplyAttack.pas:400`, write `$005B2638`) and
`ATStoningGaze` writes `Result.field_00` (`:405`, write `$005B274C`), the irrecoverable field.
Two calls, two buckets.

One genuine family difference, noted and out of scope: `PerformAttacks` calls
`Dealdamage(du, ...)` at `$005B37C5` *between* the two, so the death gaze's `LivingFigures(du)`
bound is already reduced by the stoning kills. The DOS loops share one unchanging `Cur_Figures`.
The calculator's modern arm already models the modern sequencing
(`combat_phases.js:954-973`, `Math.ceil(remaining / b.hp)` per step).

Reviewed by `codex exec` (GPT 5.6 Sol, high) under Method A; the review is in
`.reviews/F225.1.review-of-Claude.md`. It accepted the double-count finding, the bucket mapping,
the byte-identity claim and the fixture arithmetic, and supplied the extra citations now carried in

Two points were taken and are folded in above: the MoM 1.31 `attack_strength` abort (§3.1), which
rejected the unqualified phrase "in all three builds"; and the PRNG-independence qualification (§3),
which narrows what "independent" claims.

## 9. What this ruling does not settle

- **Roll correlation.** The engine's `2n` resistance rolls come from one PRNG state (raw
  `0x0FFA8`). Nothing here establishes their statistical independence; the calculator's IID model is
  assumed, as it is everywhere else. Settling it would need a Method B package over the PRNG and
  `Combat_Resistance_Check` (`0x98FCB`), and it would change far more than the gaze.
- **Composition under overkill.** How the readout should normalise buckets that sum past the
  clipped published total is a presentation decision, not a source question. F225.2 or a successor
  needs a stated rule.
- **Whether the death-gaze kill deserves its own rider histogram.** Same character: the engine
  gives it no separate bucket.
